How AI drama reels actually get made

The soap-opera-style short dramas flooding TikTok, Reels, and Shorts — secret heirs, revenge weddings, billionaire disguises — split into two completely different production tracks. Here's how each one works.

Two different things wearing the same look

Easy to conflate — they share the vertical format and the melodrama, not the production method.

Track 1

Real actors, real cheap

Apps like DramaBox and ReelShort run actual shoots — real actors and crews, produced fast and cheap, then localized and dubbed for other markets using AI voice tools. The footage itself isn't AI-generated.

Track 2

Fully AI-generated

What's exploding on TikTok and Reels: no cameras, no actors, no sets. The entire soap-opera look — faces, motion, voices — is synthesized. This is the workflow below.

The workflow

Five stages, usually chained across two or three separate tools — no single app does the whole job.

1

Script & story beats

An LLM writes short, formulaic episode scripts — secret-identity and revenge-plot templates — with a hook in the first 3 seconds and a cliffhanger at the end of each episode.

ChatGPTClaude
2

Character design

Reference images of one consistent "actor" — same face, same outfit, multiple angles — generated up front so every later shot can match back to them.

3

Video generation

The core step: animating those reference images into moving footage. Different engines are chosen for different strengths — multi-shot continuity, character consistency, or general shot quality.

Edit & assembly

Why the "same actor" looks so consistent

No single generator holds a character steady for an entire series on its own. Creators chain tools deliberately: one engine for raw generation, a second specifically for character-consistency (reference-image conditioning, like Kling's Elements or Runway's image-to-video mode), then CapCut for the vertical edit. The consistency comes from the pipeline, not any one model.
